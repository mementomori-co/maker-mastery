# Wood dust exposure limits and machine guarding (OSHA, NIOSH)

Applies to: both (shop safety, not printer or slicer specific)
Verified: 2026-10-03

All rules below are US workplace rules (employer to employee). They do not legally bind a home shop, but no source found loosens them for home use, so they are used here as the minimum good practice.

## Facts

### Machine guarding
- OSHA's woodworking eTool says machine-guarding violations under 29 CFR 1910.212(a)(1) and (a)(3)(ii) have topped its citation lists, and that poorly guarded woodworking machines cause lacerations, amputations, severed fingers and blindness. — [OSHA Woodworking eTool](https://www.osha.gov/etools/woodworking)
- 1910.212(a)(1): one or more guarding methods must protect the operator and others from point-of-operation hazards, in-going nip points, rotating parts, flying chips and sparks. — [29 CFR 1910.212](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.212)
- 1910.212(a)(3)(ii): a machine's point of operation must be guarded so no part of the operator's body can be in the danger zone during the cycle. — [29 CFR 1910.212](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.212)
- 1910.213(c)(1)-(3): every hand-fed circular rip saw needs (1) a hood enclosing the blade above the table and above the material, (2) a spreader so the material cannot pinch the blade or be thrown back, and (3) anti-kickback fingers or dogs. — [29 CFR 1910.213](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.213)

### Wood dust hazard and exposure limits
- OSHA: airborne wood dust from sanding and cutting can cause allergic and non-allergic respiratory symptoms and cancer; which wood types carry which risk is not clearly established. — [OSHA Wood Dust](https://www.osha.gov/wood-dust)
- OSHA reports wood dust as known to be a human carcinogen (NTP) and Group 1, carcinogenic to humans (IARC). IARC's own page was not checked. — [OSHA Wood Dust Hazards](https://www.osha.gov/wood-dust/hazards)
- OSHA PEL (wood dust treated as particulate not otherwise regulated), 8-hour TWA: 15 mg/m3 total dust, 5 mg/m3 respirable fraction. The same OSHA page notes NIOSH recommends 1 mg/m3 total dust and ACGIH recommends 0.5 mg/m3 for western red cedar. — [OSHA eTool: Wood Dust](https://www.osha.gov/etools/woodworking/rough-mill/wood-dust), [OSHA Table Z-1](https://www.osha.gov/annotated-pels/table-z-1)
- OSHA's wood-dust standards list: 1910.1000 (air contaminants), 1910.134 (respiratory protection), 1910.1200 (hazard communication), 1910.307 (hazardous locations), Subpart I (PPE). — [OSHA Wood Dust Standards](https://www.osha.gov/wood-dust/standards)
- NIOSH Pocket Guide, wood dust: REL TWA 1 mg/m3 with the potential-carcinogen ("Ca") flag; symptoms include eye irritation, nosebleed, dermatitis, respiratory hypersensitivity, asthma, cough, sinusitis; targets are eyes, skin and respiratory system. — [NIOSH NPG: Wood dust](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/npg/npgd0667.html)
- NIOSH respirator table for wood dust: because of the "Ca" flag, at any detectable concentration it lists only full-facepiece pressure-demand SCBA or supplied-air with auxiliary SCBA (APF 10,000). Full-facepiece N100/R100/P100 respirators (APF 50) appear only under "Escape". N95 is not listed. — [NIOSH NPG: Wood dust](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/npg/npgd0667.html)

### Noise
- 1910.95(c)(2): an 8-hour TWA of 85 dB (or a 50% dose) is the action level for the hearing conservation program. — [29 CFR 1910.95](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.95)

## In practice
- Use the three rip-saw guard features (hood, spreader, anti-kickback pawls) as a checklist for any hand-fed circular saw.
- Size dust collection to the stricter NIOSH REL of 1 mg/m3, not OSHA's 15/5 mg/m3. Because NIOSH's own table does not accept a filtering mask as the working-level control for wood dust, engineering controls (collection at the tool, extraction, wet methods) come first; a P100 mask is an extra layer, not the main control.
- Wear hearing protection for sustained noisy tools (planers, routers, shop vacuums); 85 dBA over 8 hours is the workplace action level.

## Not verified / open questions
- IARC's own Group 1 page was not checked; only OSHA's citation of it.
- No home-shop-specific wood-dust guidance was found; all limits are workplace figures.
