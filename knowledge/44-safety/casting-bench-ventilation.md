# Casting-bench ventilation and respiratory protection

Applies to: both (shop safety, not printer or slicer specific) · platinum silicone, MDI-based urethane resin, epoxy resins and coatings, aerosol release agents and primers
Verified: 2026-10-03

The products named below are examples of common hobby casting and finishing materials whose SDSs were read. They are not recommendations. Always read the current SDS for the exact product and revision you use; SDS wording changes between revisions.

## Facts

### Engineering controls come before respirators
- OSHA 1910.134(a)(1): the primary aim is to prevent contaminated air, as far as feasible by engineering controls (enclosure, general and local ventilation, substituting less toxic materials); respirators are used when such controls are not feasible or while they are being put in place. This binds employers, not a home bench; it is used here as the best-practice hierarchy. — [29 CFR 1910.134](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.134)
- NIOSH (archived 2014 page): engineering controls such as closed systems and ventilation should be the principal method for minimising isocyanate exposure, with PPE as a supplement. The live page could not be fetched, so this is not confirmed current. — [archived NIOSH isocyanates page](https://archive.cdc.gov/www_cdc_gov/niosh/topics/isocyanates/default.html)
- 1910.134(d)(1): a respirator must be NIOSH-certified and selected for the specific hazard (employer duty; used here as the selection rule). — [29 CFR 1910.134](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.134)

### Example: MDI-based urethane casting resin (Smooth-Cast 305, Part A)
- Part A hazard statements: H332 harmful if inhaled, H334 may cause allergy or asthma symptoms if inhaled, H335 respiratory irritation, H351 suspected of causing cancer. SDS revision date 2021-01-03 (the printed version number is inconsistent inside the document; cite the date). — [Smooth-Cast 305/310 SDS](https://www.smooth-on.com/msds/files/Smooth-Cast_305_310.pdf)
- Precautions: P271 use only outdoors or in a well-ventilated area; P284 wear respiratory protection if ventilation is inadequate. — [Smooth-Cast 305/310 SDS](https://www.smooth-on.com/msds/files/Smooth-Cast_305_310.pdf)
- The SDS says respiratory protection is not normally required **with adequate local exhaust ventilation**; otherwise approved respirators with suitable cartridges are a backup to engineering controls. An open window or room fan is not local exhaust. — [Smooth-Cast 305/310 SDS](https://www.smooth-on.com/msds/files/Smooth-Cast_305_310.pdf)
- The SDS precautions are for room-temperature handling; heated use or spraying may need more. — [Smooth-Cast 305/310 SDS](https://www.smooth-on.com/msds/files/Smooth-Cast_305_310.pdf)
- Part A contains 15-35% MDI (CAS 101-68-8). Limits as printed on the SDS: ACGIH TWA 0.005 ppm; OSHA Z-1 ceiling 0.02 ppm (0.2 mg/m3), a ceiling not a TWA; NIOSH REL TWA 0.005 ppm (0.05 mg/m3). Not re-checked against current tables. — [Smooth-Cast 305/310 SDS](https://www.smooth-on.com/msds/files/Smooth-Cast_305_310.pdf)
- Part B is not classified hazardous under OSHA HazCom; its California Prop 65 warning comes from a plasticiser (DINP), not MDI. — [Smooth-Cast 305/310 SDS](https://www.smooth-on.com/msds/files/Smooth-Cast_305_310.pdf)
- OSHA: isocyanate exposure irritates skin and mucous membranes and causes chest tightness and breathing difficulty; the main effects are occupational asthma and other lung problems. — [OSHA isocyanates](https://www.osha.gov/isocyanates)

### Spraying MDI needs supplied air
- NIOSH Alert 2006-149 (written for spraying MDI products such as truck-bed liners, not for poured resin): when spraying MDI, use a full-facepiece supplied-air respirator in pressure-demand or other positive-pressure mode, plus PPE. — [NIOSH Alert 2006-149](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/docs/2006-149/pdfs/2006-149.pdf)
- For re-entering the spray area after spraying stops, the minimum is a full-facepiece air-purifying respirator with combined organic-vapour/N95 cartridges (full face, not half mask). — [NIOSH Alert 2006-149](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/docs/2006-149/pdfs/2006-149.pdf)

### Sizing a capture hood (OSHA Technical Manual)
- Rule of thumb for a simple capture hood: keep the source within 1.5 duct diameters of the hood (9 in for a 6 in duct) and capture velocity at or above 50 fpm. — [OSHA Technical Manual Sec. III Ch. 3](https://www.osha.gov/otm/section-3-health-hazards/chapter-3)
- Capture velocity drops fast with distance: with 3,000 fpm in the duct, about 300 fpm at one duct diameter and about 30 fpm at two (plain or narrow-flanged hood). Two diameters from a 6 in hood is already below 50 fpm. — [OSHA Technical Manual Sec. III Ch. 3](https://www.osha.gov/otm/section-3-health-hazards/chapter-3)

### Portable air cleaners do not replace source capture (EPA)
- To filter gases, EPA advises choosing a portable cleaner with activated carbon or another gas filter, notes there is no widely used performance rating for gas filters, and says CADR covers particles only; thicker carbon beds work better. This is home air-cleaner guidance, not a substitute for a capture hood. — [EPA Guide to Air Cleaners in the Home](https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq/guide-air-cleaners-home)
- High CADR is often achieved with HEPA filters, which are rated for particles. — [EPA Guide to Air Cleaners in the Home](https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq/guide-air-cleaners-home)

### Art-material labelling (context only)
- US LHAMA (16 CFR 1500.14(b)(8)) requires chronic-hazard labelling on art materials and an ASTM D-4236 conformance statement. It is a labelling rule, not an exposure limit or ventilation rule. — [CPSC Art Materials](https://www.cpsc.gov/Business--Manufacturing/Business-Education/Business-Guidance/Art-Materials)

### Example: platinum silicone (Mold Star series)
- Smooth-On's online Part A SDS (11 Jun 2025) states the product is not a hazardous substance or mixture under GHS and not classified, and says to keep containers tightly closed in a dry, cool, well-ventilated place. — [Mold Star Part A SDS](https://www.smooth-on.com/sds/10002305.pdf)
- Part B wording, Section 8 controls and flash point were read from a non-public copy and are not verified (no public source) here; read the current Part B SDS before relying on them.

### Example: epoxy casting resin (EpoxAcast 690)
- Part A: skin irritant (H315), skin sensitiser (H317), eye irritant (H319), aquatic chronic (H411); signal word Warning. Revision date 25 Oct 2023 (version number inconsistent inside the document). — [EpoxAcast 690 SDS](https://www.smooth-on.com/msds/files/EpoxAcast_690.pdf)
- Part B is more severe: skin corrosion 1B (H314), serious eye damage 1 (H318), aquatic chronic 3 (H412); signal word Danger; corrosive to skin and eyes in animal tests. — [EpoxAcast 690 SDS](https://www.smooth-on.com/msds/files/EpoxAcast_690.pdf)
- Both parts: avoid breathing vapours; respirator not normally needed **with adequate local exhaust**, otherwise approved cartridges as backup; wear liquid-tight gloves such as butyl rubber, neoprene or PVC. — [EpoxAcast 690 SDS](https://www.smooth-on.com/msds/files/EpoxAcast_690.pdf)
- The Part B Prop 65 warning names propylene oxide but prints a CAS number that does not match it (see open questions). — [EpoxAcast 690 SDS](https://www.smooth-on.com/msds/files/EpoxAcast_690.pdf)

### Example: epoxy print coating (XTC-3D)
- Part A: skin irritant, skin sensitiser, eye irritant; signal word Warning; same local-exhaust respirator condition and butyl/neoprene/PVC glove advice. — [XTC-3D SDS](https://www.smooth-on.com/msds/files/XTC-3D.pdf)
- Part B (signal word Danger) is far more hazardous: harmful if swallowed or inhaled, causes skin burns and serious eye damage, may irritate the respiratory tract, suspected of damaging fertility or the unborn child, very toxic to aquatic life. The SDS warns of possible delayed lung injury and respiratory-tract burns from inhalation. It contains 10-30% benzene-1,3-dimethanamine (CAS 1477-55-0) with a ceiling limit of 0.1 mg/m3 (ACGIH, NIOSH, OSHA Z1A as printed). — [XTC-3D SDS](https://www.smooth-on.com/msds/files/XTC-3D.pdf)

### Example: aerosol mould release (Ease Release 200)
- Section 2: flammable aerosol category 2 (H223) and pressurised aerosol (H229), signal word Warning; keep away from heat, sparks and flames, no smoking (P210); do not spray on a flame (P211). Propellants: dimethyl ether 25-50% and 1,1-difluoroethane 25-50%. — [ER200 SDS](https://www.smooth-on.com/msds/files/ER200.pdf)
- Section 9 of the same SDS calls it a nonflammable aerosol with flash point above 300 °F, contradicting Section 2 with no explanation. Treat the can as flammable. — [ER200 SDS](https://www.smooth-on.com/msds/files/ER200.pdf)
- Store cool, dry and ventilated away from heat, sunlight and oxidisers; respirator not normally needed **with adequate local exhaust**; spray use may need added precautions. — [ER200 SDS](https://www.smooth-on.com/msds/files/ER200.pdf)

### Example: aerosol filler primer (Rust-Oleum 249279)
- Signal word Danger: extremely flammable aerosol (H222), may burst if heated (H229), causes serious eye irritation (H319), may cause drowsiness (H336), suspected of causing cancer (H351), organ damage from prolonged exposure (H373). Revision 20 Nov 2025. — [Rust-Oleum 249279 SDS](https://www.rustoleum.com/msds/english/249279.pdf)
- Flash point below -7 °C (20 °F) per the fire-hazard section (Section 9 lists -96 °C). Use only outdoors or in a well-ventilated area (P271); no ignition sources (P210). — [Rust-Oleum 249279 SDS](https://www.rustoleum.com/msds/english/249279.pdf)
- Where concentrations may exceed limits, an approved organic-vapour respirator under a respiratory protection program may be permissible; wear impervious gloves, eye and face protection. Ingredients include acetone 10-30%, propane 10-30%, n-butane 5-10%, xylenes 3-7% and ethylbenzene 0.5-1.5%. Methylene chloride is not listed. — [Rust-Oleum 249279 SDS](https://www.rustoleum.com/msds/english/249279.pdf)

### Acetone reference values (NIOSH Pocket Guide)
- NIOSH REL TWA 250 ppm; OSHA PEL TWA 1000 ppm; IDLH 2500 ppm (10% LEL). Symptoms: eye, nose and throat irritation, headache, dizziness, CNS depression, dermatitis. Up to 2500 ppm NIOSH lists an organic-vapour cartridge respirator or supplied-air respirator (APF 10). — [NIOSH NPG: Acetone](https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/npg/npgd0004.html)

### Spraying areas (OSHA 1910.107, workplace definitions)
- A spraying area is any area where dangerous amounts of flammable vapour, mist or combustible residue are present from spraying; a spray booth is a power-ventilated structure that confines spray and directs it to an exhaust. Guidance only for a home shop. — [29 CFR 1910.107](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.107)

### EPA VOC guidance
- Increase ventilation when using VOC-emitting products, follow label precautions, and when a label says "well-ventilated area" work outdoors or with an exhaust fan, otherwise open windows for as much outdoor air as possible. EPA's separate methylene-chloride caution applies to products that contain it (strippers, some spray paints); neither aerosol SDS above lists it. — [EPA: VOCs and indoor air](https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq/volatile-organic-compounds-impact-indoor-air-quality)

## In practice
- Every "respirator not normally required" line above depends on **adequate local exhaust ventilation**: a capture hood pulling vapour away at the source. A window or box fan does not meet that condition. Check your hood against the 50 fpm / 1.5-duct-diameter rule before treating urethane, epoxy or aerosol work as respirator-optional.
- Never spray MDI-based products without full-facepiece supplied air; a half-mask cartridge respirator is not adequate for spraying.
- A room air cleaner, even with HEPA, does not replace source capture for solvent or isocyanate vapour.
- Gloves: the epoxy SDSs name butyl rubber, neoprene or PVC. Do not assume nitrile is adequate because it is common; no SDS here names it.
- Treat both aerosols as flammable: no flames, sparks or heaters nearby, regardless of any contradictory line in the SDS.
- Isocyanate and amine-hardener sensitisation can be permanent; keep skin contact and inhalation to a minimum from the first use.

## Not verified / open questions
- The live NIOSH isocyanates page could not be fetched; the engineering-controls statement rests on the 2014 archive.
- The MDI limits are as printed on a 2021 SDS, not re-checked against current ACGIH/OSHA tables.
- Whether a half-mask organic-vapour/P100 respirator is adequate for hand-mixing and pouring small batches of MDI resin with local exhaust: no source checked answers this directly. Take the conservative route (better exhaust, full-face protection) until you have a qualified answer.
- EpoxAcast 690 Part B prints CAS 75-56-5 for propylene oxide; the usual CAS for propylene oxide is 75-56-9. Not verified against an authoritative CAS lookup.
- Mold Star Part B details: not verified (no public source read).
